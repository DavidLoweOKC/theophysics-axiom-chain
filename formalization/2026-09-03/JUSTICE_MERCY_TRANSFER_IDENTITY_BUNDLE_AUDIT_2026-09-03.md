# Justice, Mercy, Cost Transfer, and Identity — Three-Gauntlet Audit

**Date:** 2026-09-03  
**Result:** All three Lean files compile successfully.

## Justice and mercy

The model gives a concrete witness in which the debt remains truthfully stated and fully paid while someone other than the debtor bears the cost. Therefore justice and mercy are logically compatible in the declared accounting model.

Negative controls show that denying the debt is not justice, changing the bearer without payment is not justice, and justice alone does not entail mercy.

## Cost-bearing transfer

A valid transfer was declared to require preserved debt identity, authorization, willingness, and adequate payment. Lean verified the positive case and independently broke the transfer when each condition was removed. Adequacy alone permits overpayment, so exact equality is an additional rule rather than a consequence of sufficiency.

## Restored identity

The abstract restoration operation can substantially change integrity, relational health, and life status while preserving a declared personal identifier. However, matching all restored properties does not entail numerical identity, and identifier continuity alone does not entail memory continuity.

## Strongest conclusion

The three structures are jointly coherent:

`truthful debt + adequate authorized willing substitute → justice-compatible mercy`

`restoration can transform properties while preserving identity`

But the model does not establish that substitution is morally legitimate merely because it is mathematically representable, nor does a preserved numeric identifier prove real personal or conscious continuity. Those are the next bridge burdens.
